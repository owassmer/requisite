# 000-3 Stability, tie-robust relevance, feasible coverage, three-valued counts

## Why
Requisite's thesis is that how specific a question must be is measured, not judged by taste: a question is refined until
independent judge configurations agree on it (stability) and no further than any decision needs (relevance), and a
contract is tested against scenarios that each show one condition's independent effect (MC/DC coverage). Each of these
is easy to compute subtly wrong in a way that hides exactly what it exists to find: a top-answer score that cannot see
probability instability, a "decision flip" that is only the solver breaking a tie, a coverage obligation met by an
impossible scenario, an unknown day counted as false. The demo beats 2 and 4 (spec §15) rest on these kernels.

## What to build
Pure functions, no provider calls, no database:
- `requisite/stability/scores.py`: S_arg and S_prob as the oracle's `stability` computes them, over configurations with
  equal weight: they are perturbation diagnostics (A01), and S_prob is one minus the mean pairwise Jensen–Shannon
  divergence in bits, not the square-root distance. Zero probabilities are handled. The oracle refuses tied top answers
  and asks for an explicit policy, so provide one, named and versioned: a configuration whose top answers tie (within
  the oracle's 1e-12) contributes 1/t to each of its t tied answers, never one chosen by list order. Separately, the
  pooled distribution (addendum §2.3) under a named, versioned pooling policy, by default equal weight per provider
  split among its configurations, with the per-configuration distributions kept beside it so disagreement stays
  inspectable. Distributions that do not sum to one within the oracle's 1e-10 are an error, never normalized; turning a
  provider's reported probabilities into a distribution is the provider layer's work, not the kernel's.
- `requisite/relevance/regret.py`: min-max conditional regret over feasible answers and a finite action menu, with an
  economic tolerance ε, returning the regret and the common acceptable action(s) (addendum §3.3).
- `requisite/fuzz/coverage.py`: MC/DC witnesses for a boolean trigger over atomic conditions (spec §8.6 asks for one per
  atomic condition) under world constraints, feasible only (addendum §10.2). Trigger and constraints arrive as a small
  boolean expression form defined in this module (variables for the atomic conditions, Not, And, Or, and the
  constraints as expressions over the same variables). It is local to the kernel, and the contract IR, which the
  coordinator writes, lowers into it. It is the form python-sat can encode, so enumerate for small N and use python-sat
  above that, with the size where it takes over yours to choose and injectable so tests can force either path.
  At the level of atomic conditions the account is exact, with no third case. For condition x_i, either a feasible
  unique-cause pair exists, or none does. "None does" means, by its definition, that on the feasible states the trigger
  is a function of the other conditions: any two feasible states that agree on all but x_i give the same outcome. That
  is what "redundant under the stated constraints" says (A09): the condition has no independent effect under the
  constraints. A linked threshold or a repeated predicate is one more case of this, through the constraints. Each result
  keeps the whole account: the witness pair, or the proof of redundancy (the exhaustive search or an UNSAT result),
  since a trigger that carries a condition with no effect under its own constraints is a drafting finding about the
  contract. Redundancy is per condition, given the others, and the account must never tell a drafter that conditions are
  removable when removing them together changes the trigger. So the result reports, completely, the minimal groups of
  redundant conditions whose joint removal changes the trigger on the feasible states: minimal sets S of redundant
  conditions such that the trigger is not a function of the conditions outside S. A == B under A ∧ B gives the group
  {A, B}; A == B == C under A ∧ B ∧ C gives {A, B, C}. The search for groups is exponential only in the number of
  redundant conditions, which is small for any real trigger, and is always run to completion. Two findings about the
  whole trigger come first and are stated as such: constraints with no feasible state, and a trigger constant on the
  feasible states (it never fires, or always fires). Either makes every per-condition statement vacuous, so neither is
  reported as conditions being removable. A boundary point the constraints exclude is recorded as infeasible with its
  reason. Given a set of scenarios, the kernel also says which obligations that set meets and which it does not (A09:
  store uncovered obligations explicitly). Plus boundary values for numeric and temporal comparisons (threshold at
  declared precision, cutoff at the exact inclusive or exclusive instant and its neighbours, counts k − 1, k, k + 1).
- `requisite/contract/counts.py`: a CountTrue over three-valued daily values returns the interval [k, k + u].
- `requisite/library/reliability.py`: the exact one-sided binomial upper bound on an error rate given errors and
  independent cases (three flawless cases give 0.6316 at 95%).
These are kernels over plain values. Three-valued inputs are Python `True`, `False` and `None`, where `None` is the
unknown of spec §6.3 (Kleene), propagated and never coerced. Intervals are pairs of numbers, and each kernel returns a
plain frozen result of its own. The definition's types (the Kleene value, an obligation's status, a stability record)
are written once by the coordinator in `requisite/core/` and built from your results, so define nothing that claims to
be one. A threshold's declared precision and a cutoff's inclusivity come from the caller with the comparison, as the
contract IR will carry them. The size at which enumeration gives way to python-sat (in `uv.lock`) is your choice; say
why.
