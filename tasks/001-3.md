# 001-3 Settlement language tested before it is priced: the resolvability fuzzer

## Why
A contract is a question addressed to a future resolver, so Requisite tests it now (spec §8.6): code decides which
scenarios matter, agents make them realistic, decision models test whether the prose is resolvable, and code scores the
result. The rendering check is never cut (spec §13): a packet whose judges do not recover the scenario it was meant to
encode is the rendering's fault, fixed before any clause is blamed, so a failed resolution always points at the
contract's wording. Gate A (Mon 10:00 ET) is "an F1 contract parsed to IR, the evaluator passing, one fuzz run end to
end"; outcome 005 shows a clause failing on ambiguity, its revision, and the re-run passing.

## What to build
In `requisite/fuzz/run.py`, a fuzz run of one contract version: its IR and its prose (a ProseDraft, the clause
drafter's sections with the IR nodes each states), against a set of judge configurations, producing a committed
core.FuzzReport. The steps are spec §8.6's:
1. Coverage (code): the IR lowered by the evaluator's lowering into the coverage kernel; the scenarios that meet every
   feasible obligation (each condition's independent effect, each coupled group's joint effect) and every feasible
   boundary point, as few as do so; each obligation's status recorded (met, uncovered, redundant with its proof,
   vacuous) and each boundary point's feasibility.
2. Stress classes (code): scenarios for conflicting sources, a revised value, a renamed or recodified instrument,
   partial scope, a stay or injunction, and evidence missing at expiry, wherever the IR has the observables, sources,
   revision policy or interpretive conditions they bear on.
3. Each scenario made concrete: SyntheticObservations and interpretations consistent with its assignment, and its IR
   outcome and dated transfers from the evaluator (I8: never computed here).
4. Rendering (agent): the scenario_renderer's packet, committed in a synthetic case as SyntheticArtifacts and
   SyntheticClaims (I4).
5. Rendering check (decision models): each atomic condition the scenario fixes asked of the packet, as a question with
   answers true, false and not_established (an Interpretive condition asks its own template's question); where the
   judges' modal answers do not recover the intended assignment, the packet is re-rendered with what was not recovered,
   up to three times, and a packet that still fails is recorded as rendering_passed false and excluded from scoring.
6. Resolution (decision models): K configurations (providers × three paraphrases of the resolver instruction) answer,
   given the prose and the packet, whether the contract resolves YES, NO, or is AMBIGUOUS (and VOID or EXTENDED where
   its procedure provides them), as a library template whose paraphrases the coordinator's paraphrase agent writes.
7. Scoring (code): fidelity (the modal answer equals the IR outcome), clarity (S_arg across configurations), ambiguity
   (mean probability on AMBIGUOUS), over the scenarios whose rendering passed, against spec §8.6's starting thresholds;
   the prose's dated transfers for its outcome from the evaluator, compared with the IR's (core.results.same_transfers).
8. Attribution: each scenario tagged with the prose sections that state the conditions it exercises (the sections'
   ir_nodes); a divergence opens an Attribution naming its suspected cause and the clause, open until checked (A09
   §10.1); the hard gates of A09 §10.4 reported; the equivalence the report claims is the one its scenarios support.
Development and held-out scenarios are split by a seeded rule before anything is scored (A09 §10.4).
A run is a function the CLI calls: `uv run requisite fuzz <contract> --case <dir>`.

## Done
Tests in `tests/fuzz/`, each marked with `@pytest.mark.covers(...)` for the registry items it is the one test for:
- WP9.rendering_check: a packet that does not encode its scenario (seeded) is caught by the check, re-rendered, and
  never counted against the contract.
- A.T18: a rendering check that passes while the resolvers disagree with the IR leaves the cause open, not attributed
  to a clause.
- WP9.ambiguity_attributed: an injected undefined term in one section raises ambiguity over the threshold on the
  scenarios exercising it, and the attribution names that section.
These run on recorded responses (cassettes) and on test doubles of the providers and agents where a test needs a
response no provider gave (a test double is the test's own input, never a cassette). WP9.fidelity_f1 is a claim about
the live F1 run, which only the coordinator can record (it needs the network); the coordinator covers it with a test
that replays that run, so it is not yours. `gates` passes.
