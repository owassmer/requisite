# 001-1 The single settlement function: the three-valued IR evaluator

## Why
A contract is a question addressed to a future resolver. Requisite tests its settlement language now against its own
formal representation (the IR), and that only means something if one function says what the IR settles to, everywhere:
payoffs in construction, the examples printed in the rulebook, ground truth in fuzzing, and settlement at resolution
(spec I8, never cut). It must keep unknown as unknown (I6, never cut): a required condition that is unknown can never
produce a definitive Yes, an unknown count is an interval, and a contract's rule for an unknown at expiry decides the
procedure's outcome without rewriting the trigger's truth. Gate A (Mon 10:00 ET) is "an F1 contract parsed to IR, the
evaluator passing, one fuzz run end to end"; outcomes 001, 004 and 005 rest on this evaluator.

## What to build
In `requisite/contract/evaluate.py`: one function, `evaluate(ir, observations, interpretations, *, at) ->
ResolutionEvaluation`, and nothing else in the product computes what a contract settles to.
- Observations: the definition's `core.Observation` records (each observable's values as published, per source, with
  publication time and finality), so that the revision policy (first print, as of expiry, final with its deadline or fallback) and the conflict policy
  (precedence_first_available, require_agreement) select the value deterministically. A value not published by the
  instant it is needed is unknown. Numeric comparisons are made at the observable's declared precision.
- Interpretations: each Interpretive node's truth, from the resolved judgments of its question (core.kleene.from_status;
  contested and not_established are unknown), supplied by the caller. The evaluator never asks a model anything.
- Numbers: ValueAt reads a numeric observable's value at an instant by the same policies; ReadValue is the band of its
  resolved answer (the caller supplies it as an interval, the whole range where unknown); Computed calls its registered
  node (requisite/compute/registry.py, spec §4 @node) on its inputs' values, and over interval inputs its value is the
  node at the corners of the box where every open input is declared monotone, and unknown otherwise. Register the
  Western Grazers Net Monthly Cost computation as such a node, from its terms, for the fixture below.
- Temporal operators in Kleene logic over the observations: By (with its lower bound and deadline inclusion), At,
  Throughout, CountTrue as an interval [k, k + u] through counts.count_true, DurationIn in its unit, NumCmp over an
  interval (true where every value in the interval satisfies it, false where none does, unknown otherwise).
- Procedure: trigger truth to procedure status by the contract's rules (on_unknown_at_expiry, its extension and what
  follows, void with its transfer, disputed where require_agreement's sources disagree), keeping trigger_truth as it is
  (A05 §5.2). `at` is the instant of evaluation: before expiration, an unknown trigger is pending. Where the procedure's
  early_expiration is "when_true", expiration moves to the first instant the trigger is true on the observations then
  published, and the contract resolves there.
- Transfers: per unit, dated, in the contract's currency: the payout by kind (binary, per_unit, capped_linear) on its
  quantity, rounded by the contract's quantum and mode, paid at expiration plus the settlement and payment lags in the
  contract's day count and time zone (business days skip weekends and the named holiday calendar's holidays: the one
  registered, us_federal, is 5 U.S.C. §6103's ten holidays and Inauguration Day with their observed-day rules, computed
  by rule in requisite/contract/calendar.py rather than listed), or per period for a per-period payment. Where the quantity is an interval at settlement, the transfer is not a guess: the
  procedure's missing-data rule decides, and otherwise the result is unresolved, saying on what.
- Lowering for coverage: the trigger's boolean structure (And, Or, Not) over its atomic conditions (every other node:
  Cmp, Interpretive, NumCmp, By, At, Throughout; a NumCmp over a Computed or ReadValue is one atomic condition), as the
  coverage kernel's expression form, one variable per atomic
  condition named by its IR node id, with the boundary points of each comparison (threshold at declared precision,
  cutoff instants at the comparison's resolution, counts k - 1, k, k + 1).

## Done
Tests in `tests/contract/`, each marked with `@pytest.mark.covers(...)` for the registry items it is the one test for:
- I8: a contract's examples (ScenarioExample) carry the evaluator's own outcome for their scenario, and a test that
  every module in the product that turns a contract and a scenario into an outcome or a payoff does it by calling this
  function (an import-and-call check over the source, as tests/store/test_store.py checks the database boundary).
- A.T16: an unknown trigger that defaults to NO at expiry keeps trigger_truth unknown, with procedure resolved_no and
  the policy recorded.
- A.T17: unknown observations in a count give an interval or unknown numeric result, never false or zero.
- A.T06: a payout that matches the loss in total but arrives after the cash loss: the evaluator's dated transfers show
  when it arrives (the liquidity consequence is the optimizer's; the dates are the evaluator's).
Property tests (hypothesis) at the limits: the trigger's truth is monotone in information (replacing an unknown
observation or interpretation with a value never turns a true or false into its opposite; a second source's value that
disagrees under require_agreement makes the procedure disputed, a distinct state, and is outside this property); a
contract whose every input is known and admissible at the instant it is needed (each value published and, for a final
policy, final; each interpretation affirmative or negative; each reading a single band) never yields unknown; inclusive and exclusive cutoffs differ exactly at the cutoff instant; a business-day lag never lands on
a weekend or holiday. The Western Grazers terms expressed as an IR by hand in a test fixture (the coordinator's agents
will propose IRs from filings; this one is yours to write, from the terms, so the evaluator is proven on the real
structure) evaluate to resolved_yes when the interpretive conditions of a qualifying action are supplied as true and its Net
Monthly Cost, computed by the registered node from the action's terms (ReadValue) and the published rates (ValueAt), is
at or below the threshold, as the terms put it ("does not exceed"); and with the real market's settlement as an outside
check: the IR resolves yes by early expiration at the instant the qualifying action is evidenced, on the terms as filed
(the market settled YES on 2026-09-19; supply the qualifying action's interpretations and terms as the fixture's inputs,
and say in your report what those inputs are and where each would come from); to pending before expiration where they
are not established; and to the contract's rule at
expiration where they remain unknown. Where expressing the terms needs a construct the IR does not have, say exactly
which in your report: that is a finding about the grammar, which the coordinator fixes in the definition. `gates` passes;
`uv run requisite registry` shows the items covered. This task adds no CLI: the evaluator reaches the running product
through outcome 001's harvest and fuzz commands, which the coordinator builds on it.
