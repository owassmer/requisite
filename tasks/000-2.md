# 000-2 Wide-mode inference that matches its sampling design

## Why
In wide discovery (spec §7.2–7.4) a model labels every comment in a frozen docket cheaply and noisily, and Owen labels a
random sample by hand. Decisions use the aggregates: what share of businesses report exposure to which event family. A
naive average of model labels is biased whenever the model's errors differ across groups; the gold labels correct that
bias. But the correction is only valid for the design that actually drew the gold sample: here the gold sample is a
subset of the same frozen corpus, which is a finite-frame design, not the two independent samples the textbook PPI
formula assumes. The demo's first beat (spec §15 beat 1) shows prevalence with intervals; outcome 002 rests on this.

## What to build
In `requisite/discovery/inference.py` (create `requisite/discovery/`): pure functions over arrays and an explicit
design description.
- The finite-frame difference estimator with inclusion probabilities (Horvitz–Thompson correction of the residual),
  its SRS form and design-based standard error with the finite population correction (no Var(f)/N term), and the
  stratified form with per-stratum weights and variances (addendum §8.2, §8.4).
- Power tuning with λ: fixed λ, and λ estimated without fitting on the same gold sample it corrects (a pilot split or
  cross-fitting), with the choice reported (§8.3).
- The two-sample PPI++ mean, used only when the design says the labelled and unlabelled samples are independent draws.
- Model probabilities f in [0, 1], not top answers (spec §7.4).
The design is described by the caller (frame size, unit, strata and their sizes, sample type, inclusion probabilities);
take it as plain data here. The canonical `SamplingDesign` record (addendum §12) is defined by the coordinator in
`requisite/core/`, and will be passed to you in that shape later, so define no record that claims to be it.
