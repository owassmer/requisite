# 000-1 The liquidity optimizer and CVaR kernel

## Why
Construction chooses an instrument by what it does to the client's cash over time, not by how well it predicts. A hedge
whose payout arrives after the loss can leave the client worse off than no hedge, and a CVaR objective that is flat can
"choose" an arbitrary purchase. The demo's construction beat (spec §15 beat 3) shows the optimizer's choice and its
residual table with liquidity timing; outcome 004 rests on this kernel, and nothing else in Requisite may compute these
numbers (spec I7: code owns consequences). The build spec's own WP10 tests are partly wrong, and the mathematical
addendum (accepted by Owen) replaces them, so build to the addendum.

## What to build
In `requisite/optimize/` (a new package): pure functions over numbers, with no provider calls and no database.
- Discrete CVaR by the Rockafellar–Uryasev minimization (fractional tail mass at an atom), and a labelled
  `expected_liquidity_shortfall` = E[D] where that is what is meant (addendum §6.3).
- The liquidity LP of addendum §6.2 over scenarios × dated time grid × candidate instruments: baseline cash before the
  hedge, per-unit cumulative receipts, premium paid on a date on the grid, a reserve per date, budget and quantity caps.
  Primary solve minimizes CVaR of the maximum dated deficit; a secondary solve minimizes premium subject to the primary
  optimum within a declared solver tolerance (§6.4). Realized cash paths and deficits are recomputed independently from
  the chosen quantities, never read from LP slack. The result carries solver status, both objectives, constraint
  residuals, the monetary scale used, the tolerance, the primary solve's cash duals and budget dual, and each candidate's
  reduced cost (§7.2: μ c_j − Σ β_st A_stj), including for a candidate not in the solve (score a proposed instrument
  against the current duals). A negative reduced cost is a diagnostic, never a conclusion: the kernel re-solves with the
  candidate and reports the improvement it actually measured. Whether such an instrument exists or can be bought is not a
  statement the kernel makes, so its result carries no field that could say so (addendum §7.2, A.T10).
- Money arrives as Decimal and is scaled explicitly for the solver; quantities are rechecked through the authoritative
  cash recomputation (§6.4), done in Decimal. Use scipy's HiGHS (linprog), in `uv.lock`. The solver tolerance ε_solver of
  §6.4 is a declared parameter of each solve, absolute on the scaled primary objective, defaulting to the oracle's 1e-9 and
  reported in the result; the addendum gives no value of its own. Duals are reported in money units with the addendum's
  signs (β, μ), which the oracle's check 9 fixes: its reduced cost of −110 equals the finite difference.
This is a kernel: return a plain result of numbers. The canonical record that the runtime commits (`OptimizationResult`,
addendum §12) is defined by the coordinator in `requisite/core/` and will be built from your result, so define no
record type that claims to be it.

registry test refuses a test that covers them. Property tests (hypothesis) at the limits:
- a dominated payoff in the addendum's sense (§6.5: per-unit cumulative receipts never exceed acquisition cost in any
  state-date, no other benefit, an objective monotone in cash shortfall) is bought at zero under premium tie-breaking;
- adding a candidate never raises the primary objective by more than ε_solver;
- the LP's primary objective equals the CVaR recomputed in Decimal from the chosen quantities' cash paths, within the
  declared tolerance, and every cap and the budget hold in that recomputation.
`gates` (on your PATH) passes and `uv run requisite registry` shows those items covered.
