# 004-1 Construction: from a deep case's exposure to the contract that best protects the business

## Why
Requisite's purpose is to protect a business from a harm it can see coming and cannot otherwise insure. A pending rule
would raise its labor or input costs, and its cash could run short before relief arrives. Construction (spec §8,
outcome 004, demo beat 3) turns the deep case's exposure into a new contract: the one that best covers the business's
cash shortfall within its budget. The contract is written in the contract language, so the one settlement function
(requisite/contract/evaluate.py) says what it pays in every scenario and when. The optimizer picks quantities to
minimize the liquidity shortfall's tail, and the report shows what stays uncovered and when the cash runs short.

The business is the one Owen promotes from the wide run today (outcome 002). The deep case (task 003-1, landed in
requisite/discovery/deep.py) gives its exposure register: dated monthly cash-flow deltas per pending event, as intervals, with the
event's probability. Build construction over the register's kind, never over one case. Test it on a deep-case store
built in your tests, so it runs on the live case the moment that case exists.

## What to build
In `requisite/construct/`, behind `uv run requisite construct <step> --case-dir <dir> --case <name>`:
1. **Observables (§8.1):** candidate observables for each exposure in the register's top k. An agent proposes each
   candidate (kind "proposition" or an observable proposal, whichever the definition holds; say which). Each candidate
   gets its source hierarchy, publication schedule and revision policy. Each is judged on the hedgeability questions
   (client_can_influence, source_authoritative, timely) through the recorded provider layer.
2. **Candidates (§8.2):** contract IRs generated by code from library structure templates, over the observables that
   pass. The families are digital, time-in-state (a strip paying per period an adverse state persists: for policy risk,
   since a digital pays the same whether relief lasts a month or years), and ladder or compound where an observable
   supports them. Variants cover retroactivity, partial relief and caps. Each candidate is a ContractIR the evaluator
   accepts.
3. **Payoff matrix and price (§8.3):** for each candidate and scenario, the payoff and its settlement date from
   `evaluate` (I8). The price is a listed quote where one exists and settles on the same predicate (requisite/market,
   read-only; a quote on a different settlement predicate is not this candidate's price, spec §10 mapping). Otherwise it
   is indicative: the expected dated payout under the scenario model's probabilities, from the evaluator's transfers,
   discounted by a stated convention and times (1 + loading), labeled indicative with that convention (addendum A06
   §6.7: "For count, ladder and capped-linear structures, use expected payout, not one event probability times an
   undefined payout scalar"). Reprice over the loading range before the report says one design beats another.
   **Scenarios and their probabilities.** A probability enters only from a ProbabilityEstimate over the deep case's
   events (an event tree's branches: core.world), never from an interval's endpoints. An interval-valued cash delta is
   uncertainty about the business model, not an event. The endpoint models are two: every interval at its adverse end
   (the end that lowers cash), and every interval at its favourable end. The structure chosen is the solve under the
   all-adverse model, since protection is bought against the bad end of what is known; the favourable model's solve is
   shown beside it. Where every candidate's payoff depends on the events and not on the size of the business's own loss,
   the all-adverse model is provably the worst case over every combination of interval values (each month's deficit is
   then nondecreasing in each loss delta), and the report may say so. Where a candidate's payoff moves with the loss
   (an indemnity, a cost test), that claim is not made: the report states the two endpoint models' solves as exactly
   that, and a loss-dependent candidate's indicative price per endpoint model. Never masses assigned to endpoints, and
   never a midpoint (A04 §4.1). The business's dated cash comes once from its driver tree and liquidity
   profile, with the events' effects through it: register rows are standalone impacts and are not summed into a
   portfolio loss without the shared model (A06 §6.1: "Register rows are not automatically additive"). Where the deep
   case holds no ProbabilityEstimate for an event, the solve is refused and the missing input named, since solving over
   the events that have one would silently shrink the scenario model. An estimate with no band cannot be varied, and
   its robustness line says so.
   **Several events.** The deep case commits one ProbabilityEstimate per event (deep.estimate) and no EventTree joining
   them. With one event in the top k there is nothing to join. With several and no committed EventTree stating how they
   depend, build the joint scenarios under independence, recorded as an assumed scenario model (named in the record,
   its estimates' meaning "hypothetical" for the joint) and stated in the report beside "optimal", never silent; where
   a committed EventTree exists, its conditional branches are the joint.
   **Each scenario's cash.** Compute it through the deep case's own computation graph with the scenario's events in
   effect (deep.recompute and its registered nodes), not by summing register rows. Where that graph cannot compute a
   joint scenario, extending it is part of this task (deep.py is the product's, and this task's done depends on it).
4. **Optimization (§8.3, A06):** the liquidity-CVaR program in requisite/optimize over the business's dated cash
   (liquidity profile, operating flows and the register's loss path). It reports the chosen quantities, total premium,
   the residual and breach probability with and without the hedge, the scenario table, uncovered scenarios and timing
   gaps, and each unbought candidate's reduced cost beside its re-solve's measured improvement. Shortfall is recomputed
   independently of the LP's slack.
5. **Robustness (§8.3):** re-solve across each forecast ± its uncertainty. A forecast is an event's
   ProbabilityEstimate, varied across its stated band; the cash intervals are covered by the endpoint models above. Say whether the chosen structure holds. Where
   it flips, the responsible forecast is named as a high-priority question.
Parameters: the budget, the CVaR level α, k, the tolerance and the loading come from the case's DecisionSpec and
LiquidityProfile where the definition holds them. Where it does not, each is a parameter of the step, and its value is
recorded in the construction record and shown in the report. Optimality is stated only as the purpose phrases it: optimal within the declared
tolerance, under this evidence, candidate set, pricing policy and scenario model. Robustness varies one forecast at a
time, and the report says so. The construction report's dossier page is a later renderer (task 007-1's dossier); the
record must hold everything that page needs.
6. **The construction record:** a committed core record of all of it. core.results already holds OptimizationResult,
   PricedCandidate, CashSummary and ReducedCost; extend from them. Where the definition lacks a type or field you need,
   draft it in requisite/core in your workspace, citing its source as every core field does, and build and test against
   it. It goes through the definition review when your work lands, and the coordinator settles any change the review
   asks for. Its discount convention is yours to choose and record. The observables agent's prompt is the same: draft
   it in requisite/agents/prompts, and the coordinator reviews it at landing, before any live call uses it.

## What each record says
Every field of the construction record says exactly what produced it, so no weaker operation can fill a stronger field:
a quoted price is a quote and an indicative one says its convention; an endpoint model's solve is that model's, not an
expectation; "optimal" names the tolerance, candidate set, pricing policy and scenario model it was tested on; a
robustness result names the band and the one-at-a-time variation it covered (A06 §6.7). Where an operation establishes
less than a field means, the field is refused or narrowed, never filled.

## Done
- Tests in `tests/construct/` on deep-case stores built in the tests, with test doubles of the agent and the judges
  (never a cassette). They show:
  - every payoff in the matrix is `evaluate`'s;
  - the LP's optimum is confirmed by an independent check whose own error is stated: an exhaustive solve over a
    quantity grid agrees with it within the grid's resolution bound plus the declared tolerance (the bound computed,
    not assumed), or a second solver agrees within tolerance;
  - dated cash and deficits recomputed from the chosen quantities match the reported table;
  - on a nondegenerate fixture where the candidate is nonbasic and the step keeps the basis, its reduced cost equals
    its finite difference; in the product, each reduced cost is shown beside the re-solve's measured improvement, never
    alone (duals are not unique under degeneracy: design/decisions.md, "Reduced costs are diagnostics only");
  - a payout matching the loss in total but late shows its timing gap;
  - a forecast whose band flips the choice is named.
- WP10's registry items: each covered by exactly one test, marked.
- `uv run requisite construct ...` runs end to end on a test case: a test drives `requisite.cli.main` in process over a
  deep-case store it built, with the agent and the judges doubled. The live case runs later from recordings the
  coordinator makes.
- `gates` passes.
