# 005-1 A clause that fails the wording test is revised, and the re-run passes on scenarios it was never tuned to

## Why
A contract protects a business only if its words settle the way it means. The wording test (requisite/fuzz, landed in
001-3) has independent readers settle made-up situations from the words alone. Where they disagree with the settlement
function or hesitate, a clause is suspected. The product's promise (outcome 005, demo beat 4) is the next step:
- the suspected clause is revised;
- the test runs again and passes;
- the pass counts only on the held-out scenarios, which the revision never saw, because tuning wording to the cases you
  test on proves nothing (addendum A09; A01 §2.5).

The rulebook's published examples are then the scenarios that passed, never hand-picked (spec §8.5).

This has already happened for real. Gate A's run on KXETHD (an ETH price contract) found readers hesitating:
- on a price exactly at the strike: is $1,449.99 "above $1,449.99"?
- when no price is published.

Clarity was 0.873, against a starting bar of 0.90. That run is your real case to prove the machinery on. The demo
applies the same machinery to the contract construction builds for the business discovery finds (task 004-1, built
beside you).

## What to build
In requisite/fuzz/, behind CLI commands:
1. **`requisite fuzz revise`:** given a committed fuzz report, the clause drafter is asked to revise only the sections
   the report's open attributions suspect. Its input is that section's text, the IR nodes it states, and the evidence of
   the failing development scenarios (what the readers answered, where they hesitated). It never sees the held-out
   scenarios. The revised rulebook is proposed as a clause proposal through its gates, and it differs from the old one
   only in the suspected sections. Code checks that.
2. **The re-run** on the revised rulebook, over the same plan and the same held-out split (the split is seeded by the
   contract version, so scenarios keep their sets). The report compares the two runs: fidelity, clarity and ambiguity on
   the development and the held-out scenarios separately, before and after.
3. **Attribution closed by evidence:**
   - A suspicion is confirmed when revising its section alone turns the failing development scenarios into passing ones,
     with no other section changed.
   - Otherwise it stays open, or is refuted where the scenarios still fail with the revised section.
   - The held-out result is reported beside the development result. A pass on development that fails on held-out is
     reported as exactly that.
4. **The rulebook's examples:** the passing coverage scenarios of the final run, rendered by code with the evaluator's
   outcomes, filling the rulebook's examples section (core.Rulebook keeps it empty until then).

## Done
- Tests in tests/fuzz on test doubles of the drafter and the judges (never a cassette):
  - a seeded ambiguous clause is revised and passes on held-out;
  - a revision that touches an unsuspected section is refused;
  - a development pass with a held-out fail is reported as such;
  - the examples are exactly the passing coverage scenarios.
- On KXETHD: write in your report the commands the coordinator runs live, and what the recorded first run's report
  shows the revise step will receive.
- `gates` passes.
