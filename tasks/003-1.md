# 003-1 A deep case modelled: sensitivities, materiality computed by code, a traced register, the requisite loop

## Why
Deep discovery models one business from the inside out (spec §7.1): its P&L as drivers, how each pending event moves
each driver, and what that does to its cash over the planning horizon. Every number in its exposure register must be
computed by code from committed state and trace to the claims, judgments and nodes it rests on (WP11a), and the
requisite loop decides how specific each question needs to be: a question whose answer cannot change which exposures
enter the register's top k is informational for that decision and leaves the review queue; an unstable one is
diagnosed and, where compound, split (spec §2.1–2.3; addendum A02, A03). Outcome 003 is this case for the business
promoted from the wide run, and construction (004) and the one-change diff (006) are built on its register.

## What to build
In `requisite/discovery/deep.py`, the case's steps after its entity model passes G1:
1. Sensitivity judgments: for each driver and each pending event of the case, the deep templates' questions (direction,
   mechanism, magnitude band, lag band, conditionality) over the case's evidence state, asked in the phase-1
   configuration and, for every question that reaches the decision, in spec §2.1's stability configurations; resolved
   into core.ResolvedJudgments with their scores, pooled distributions and primary diagnosis by spec §2.1's table
   (A01 §2.4: every probe's evidence kept). A contested question is held as not established (G2).
2. Materiality: registered consequence nodes (in requisite/library/nodes/, as library additions, spec §12) that map a
   magnitude band, as an interval, through the driver's quantity × price, its period and the seasonality into dated
   monthly cash-flow deltas over the horizon, a lag band shifting their start, as intervals; combined with the event's
   probability (a ProbabilityEstimate from a forecast judgment, exact as the definition requires) into expected and
   tail impact intervals, and a three-valued breach flag against cash and credit. All of it in the computation graph
   (runtime.py), so a parameter change recomputes only what depends on it and the diff shows it (WP11a.recompute).
3. Hedgeability screen: the deep templates' observable questions for each exposure, and whether a listed market is
   close (read-only market data, requisite/market/, where task 001-2 has built it).
4. The register: core.RegisterRow per driver-event pair, every number from the graph, every row naming its claims,
   judgments and vertex ids.
5. The requisite loop (spec §2.3) over steps 1–3 with the decision "enters the top k of the register": each question's
   tie-robust relevance by min-max regret over its feasible answers, at the decision's declared tolerance (A03 §3.3,
   relevance/regret.py); a question with regret within tolerance is informational for that decision, recorded as such
   and out of the review queue, still inspectable (A02 §3.4: informational only for the named decision); an unstable
   relevant question gets its diagnosis's action (refine, fetch, split, human), a split proposed as sub-questions with
   their composition, admitted only through the split gates (stability and relevance: core.SPLIT_GATES); a stable coarse
   question that hides a decision-relevant distinction yields a core.RefinementWitness (A02 §3.2). The loop stops when
   every relevant question is stable, the budget is spent (a blocked draft, never an approved conclusion: A.T22), or a
   human must decide.
A case is run by functions the CLI calls: `uv run requisite deep <step> --case <dir>`.

## Done
Tests in `tests/discovery/`, marked with `@pytest.mark.covers(...)` for the registry items each is the one test for:
WP11a.trace (every register row traces to claims, judgments and nodes, each of which exists in the store),
WP11a.recompute (materiality recomputes on a parameter change, only what depends on it, shown by the diff),
WP7.informational (a question that reaches no change in the top-k decision is informational, out of the queue, still
inspectable), WP7.queue (the review queue orders a seeded fixture by relevance and instability, S_prob included, ties by
the objective's spread), A.T02, A.T03, A.T22, DX.base, WP4.imprecise_target, WP4.compound_split. Seeded fixtures run on
test doubles of the providers (the test's own input, never a cassette); a vague question yields imprecise_target and
splitting a compound one raises stability, as WP4 states. `gates` passes. The live case is run by the coordinator.
